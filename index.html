02 / FROM SIGNAL TO INSIGHT

What happens between the beats?

01

Record the electricity

An ECG measures voltage differences at the skin as the heart activates and recovers electrically. P waves reflect atrial activation, QRS complexes ventricular activation, and T waves ventricular recovery.

02

Bring the signal into focus

Subtract a centered 0.6-second moving average to reduce baseline drift. Apply a 35 Hz first-order low-pass filter forward and backward to reduce rapid noise without net phase shift. Filtering changes morphology and edge behavior.

03

Find candidate beats

Find local absolute peaks above a signal-derived amplitude threshold, supporting either polarity. Keep the largest candidate within a 300 ms exclusion window. Ignore the first and last 0.4 seconds to reduce edge effects.

04

Measure the spacing

Sample differences divided by sampling rate give R–R intervals. Heart rate is 60 divided by the average interval in seconds. Results use the full recording, independent of zoom.

The engineering problem

How can a noisy stream of voltage samples become an understandable view of cardiac timing? Pulse Lab connects signal processing, interactive visualization, and responsible communication in a browser-based learning tool.

Built to explore, with limits

This simplified detector is not clinically validated or benchmarked against annotated recordings. Motion artifacts, pacing, low-amplitude signals, unusual morphology, and irregular rhythms can cause incorrect detections. Quality checks are heuristics; passing them does not establish accuracy. No diagnosis or medical decisions should be based on this project.

The sample combines Gaussian-shaped waves, baseline drift, and seeded noise. It is simulated, not a patient recording. Uploads require one uniformly sampled channel, 100–2,000 Hz, at least 5 seconds, and at most 300,000 samples. Summaries are withheld if any quality check fails; candidate intervals remain available for inspection.
